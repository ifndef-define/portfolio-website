Custom Robotics Interface Board

PCB Design KiCad STM32G4 Hardware

The Problem

Our robotics team was using generic breakout boards and wiring harnesses. Every connection point was a potential failure. We had reliability issues in competition, and debugging hardware failures during matches was a nightmare. I wanted to design a single PCB that could integrate all our sensors, motor drivers, and communication interfaces reliably.

The Solution

Designed an STM32G474-based interface board (using KiCad) integrating quadrature encoders, 6-axis IMU (SPI), multi-protocol comms (UART, I2C, CAN), power regulation (5V/3.3V), reverse polarity protection, and real-time I/O. Firmware handles sensor fusion, encoder counting with hardware capture, and protocol multiplexing.

Design Highlights:

  • STM32G474 MCU: High-performance ARM Cortex-M4 with fast ADC and DMA
  • Encoder Inputs: Direct quadrature capture with hardware-based counting
  • IMU Integration: SPI-based 6-axis accelerometer/gyroscope
  • Multi-Protocol Comms: UART, I2C, and CAN for communication flexibility
  • Power Distribution: Dual buck regulators with reverse polarity protection
  • Signal Conditioning: ADC input filtering for analog sensor noise rejection

What I Learned

PCB layout matters—ground planes, trace routing, and signal integrity directly affect firmware debugging. Multi-protocol buses require careful state management. Power budgeting and thermal management must be considered from day one.

Key Insights:

  • PCB Layout: Ground planes reduce noise, critical for analog sensors
  • Trace Routing: Kept high-speed signals short and direct to SPI/I2C devices
  • Decoupling: Proper capacitor placement near power pins is non-negotiable
  • DMA Efficiency: Used STM32 DMA to offload sensor data capture from CPU
  • Prototyping Iteration: First rev had issues; learned to validate power delivery early

Results & Takeaways

The board worked reliably in competition. Hardware failures dropped significantly, and debugging issues became faster because we had a known good baseline. This experience taught me that good hardware design isn't just about features—it's about reliability, debuggability, and iteration.

Tech Stack

Microcontroller: STM32G474 (Arm Cortex-M4)
PCB Design: KiCad (Schematic, Layout, Manufacturing)
Firmware: STM32 HAL, C
Sensors: Quadrature Encoders, 6-Axis IMU (ICM-20689)
Communication: UART, I2C, CAN Bus
